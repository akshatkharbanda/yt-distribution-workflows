Are you wondering why even people who need your B2B SaaS or service still ignore it?

And not because it sucks.

Well, maybe it does, but that's a separate tragedy.

The real problem is worse.

Most people are not actively looking for a category at all.

In economics, it's called there is no inherent demand.

But in normal people language, it's called nobody cares yet.

Now compare it with a new frontier AI model that say a Chinese lab comes up with and they claim, "Hey, we are smarter than OpenAI's latest and biggest model, and we have a gigantic context window, plus we are five times cheaper."

And everybody loses their mind.

People use the model for 5 minutes on OpenRouter and post about it for 15 hours on LinkedIn and YouTube, even though half of these people abandon these models by the second week.

But why does this happen?

Because people already understand the value of frontier AI:
top-tier intelligence, lower cost, faster work, obvious upside.

OpenAI and Anthropic have already created demand for this category.

Now everyone else is competing to capture that demand.

The market moves by itself.

That is what inherent demand looks like.

But most B2B companies like us do not get that luxury.

We have lower demand and intense competition.

Most B2B products are useful, but they are not AI-level magical.

And that is okay.

Billion-dollar B2B businesses are built on good products but with strong distribution.

In fact, many companies prioritize distribution over the product, especially when buyers are extremely busy, skeptical of everyone because everyone has bombarded them with pitches with even bigger claims, and they are just mildly curious because they know once in a while some really good product comes out.

So if your category does not have natural demand, which I argue it won't, you need another strategy.

And personally, when I have been working with so many B2B companies, there is one pattern I keep seeing, especially for companies that acquire new clients and new logos consistently.

That is usually combining outbound with a channel that buyers are already paying attention to.

The biggest of them is conferences.

Secondary is partnerships or certain pieces of content based on which industry you are in.

Cold outbound by itself is hard.

Events by themselves are unpredictable and expensive.

But when you combine both of these, now outbound has a reason to exist.

Because every buyer you want is suddenly trapped inside one building for 2 days.

They are highly over-caffeinated.

They are slightly underfed.

And surprisingly, these people are now willing to listen to your pitch.

If done right, conferences can compress 6 months of your outreach, LinkedIn outreach, or cold email outreach into one afternoon.

But most companies waste this opportunity completely.

They buy a booth.

They stand there.

They get excited with a few decent conversations with really good prospective buyers.

And now they hope those conversations will magically convert themselves into pipeline.

Meanwhile, just a few centimeters away from them, tens of their ideal buyers just walk past them and disappear forever.

They were like a few inches away.

So the move that quadruples your ROI on conferences is something called pre-event outbound.

Reach out to buyers before the event.

Book the meeting before the event.

Give buyers a reason to notice you on the event day before the event.

And now the conference becomes a rapid-fire discovery call sequence, and not an intro call, and also not an expensive team getaway for your team.

And something even more interesting happens.

The buyers that show up already know who you are.

And that is a completely different conversation.

And I am not saying this out of thin air.

My clients tell me that.

Outbound works much better when it is attached to something that already partly works.

And this is the part most companies misunderstand.

Cold outbound is not just a standalone engine.

Well, it can be, but it is actually a very big force multiplier for things or channels where attention already exists, where trust already exists.

Imagine outbound for events, roundtables, partnerships, webinars, reports, things that your prospects already want.

And once you keep doing that, your buyers start to see you everywhere they are already paying attention.

In B2B, this feeling matters a lot to the buyer because buyers rarely purchase after one touchpoint.

They buy after repeated trust signals.

They buy after repeated exposure.

They buy after they see you everywhere.

You can orchestrate that.

Most B2B companies do not fail because the product is terrible.

That can be, but most times the market has not paid enough attention to them.

Which is why the real game is not send more emails or do more calls.

The real game is to manufacture enough attention and trust that buyers start conversations themselves.

They perceive you as valuable by showcasing whatever you have done in multiple ways and multiple channels.

And by the way, this is literally what my company BizAmps helps other B2B companies build:
partnerships, conferences, and outbound combined into one coordinated demand engine.

Which is not just a separate marketing tragedy.
