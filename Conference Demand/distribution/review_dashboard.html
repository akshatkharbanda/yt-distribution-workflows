Punchy/Funny

Most companies do conferences like this: Buy booth. Print banners. Fly the team out. Stand there with polite smiles. Then hope pipeline appears. This is not a strategy. It is an expensive team outing with event badges. Meanwhile, the exact buyers they want walk a few feet away, grab coffee, say "interesting", and disappear forever. The better move is pre-event outbound. Reach out before the event. Book meetings before the event. Give buyers a reason to notice you before they walk past your booth. Cold outbound alone is hard. Events alone are unpredictable. Together, outbound has context and the event has intent. Watch the full video.

Problem-led

Your buyer can need your product and still ignore you. Not because the product is useless. Because there is no inherent demand. Normal people translation: Nobody cares yet. That is why cold outbound feels so brutal for many B2B teams. Conferences help because attention is already concentrated. For two days, the people you want are in one building. But the conference alone will not save you. If you wait until the event day, you are competing with booths, coffee lines, badge scans, tote bags, and everyone's tired feet. The move is to warm the room before you enter it. Watch the full video.

Tactical

If you are sponsoring or attending a B2B conference, do not start outreach on event day. Start before the event. Three moves: 1. Build a named account list of buyers who are likely to attend. 2. Reach out with a conference-specific reason, not a generic "quick call?" message. 3. Book short meetings before everyone lands at the venue. Now the event is not a cold start. People already know who you are. They know why you are relevant. They have a reason to stop. Cold outbound alone is hard. Events alone are unpredictable. Together, outbound becomes the multiplier. Contrarian

Cold outbound is not always the engine. Sometimes it is the multiplier. That distinction matters. If there is no demand, no trust, no timing, and no context, outbound has to do too much work. It has to create attention, explain the problem, build credibility, and ask for time. But attach outbound to something buyers already care about and the job changes. Conferences. Partnerships. Reports. Webinars. Now outbound is not shouting into a quiet room. The event creates intent. Outbound creates the meeting. Watch the full video.

Founder Lesson

One hard founder lesson: Useful does not automatically mean wanted. Most B2B products are useful. That is not enough. Buyers are busy, skeptical, and already buried under claims from vendors who all sound painfully similar. So the job is not just: "Send more emails." The job is: Manufacture enough attention and trust that buyers start conversations themselves. Conferences are one way to do that. Not because booths are magic. Because your buyers are already paying attention to the category, the event, and the problems around it. Watch the full video.
